# The visual world is a mid-century magazine identity program

The site is designed as a magazine identity program scaled to a single reading column: warm paper and dark ink in both colour schemes, one spot colour, a strict two-column grid with a wayfinding rail on the left, a heavy rule above the words, a drop initial, and a small mark. The mark is a plumb bob, drawn once in one colour, placed once per page in a different position each time, and never locked up with the name. There is no name header, no hero, no wordmark; the home paragraph carries the name. Locked 2026-09-22 after eleven direction rounds. Durable tokens and rules live in `DESIGN.md`.

## Considered options

Rejected on the way, in order: saturated colour planes and boards (jarring); a single typeface everywhere (plain); cream paper with a centred serif column and vermilion accents (too plain, the category default); layout variants and single-lever tweaks of one direction (incoherent); a saturated green field in the software-marketing register and a photocopied zine with enlarged headings (this is not a marketing site; legibility and the reading experience come first, not large hero type).

## Consequences

- The built world is the incumbent. Refinements inherit it; the direction is not reopened without a new ADR.
- Bold means a whole coherent system at reading scale, never scale or colour for its own sake.
- Hierarchy is drawn with rules of three weights and with type. No shadows, radii, cards, tints, pills, or buttons.
